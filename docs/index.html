EV charging gaps Comunidad de Madrid

Where is public charging thin relative to demand and to what similar municipalities have? A screening tool for a charge-point operator, at municipality level.

The opportunity score combines six percentiles with a weighted geometric mean, so a municipality needs both demand and undersupply to rank high. Move the weights to see how the top 20 changes. Only municipalities with ≥ 5,000 residents are scored.

    • Overdispersed counts. Pearson dispersion under Poisson is 12.4; the negative binomial (α ≈ 0.80) is clearly preferred. Poisson intervals are shown for comparison and are far too narrow.
    • Fuel-station density is the only robust predictor (+48% points per resident per SD). Income is borderline (p ≈ 0.06).
    • Low explanatory power (pseudo-R² ≈ 0.03): supply looks driven by operator decisions more than local fundamentals.

    What this shows

    Each of the 179 municipalities in the Comunidad de Madrid is compared on public charging supply, resident EV demand and the supply a count model expects for a municipality like it. The opportunity score turns those into a 0–100 ranking for where a new public station is most needed.

    Score components

    • Undersupply (0.60): scarcity, resident EVs per public point (0.25); missing points vs. the model (0.20); observed ÷ expected (0.15).
    • Demand (0.30): resident EVs, fleet-corrected (0.20); population density as a proxy for homes without a garage (0.10).
    • Feasibility (0.10): fuel stations as conversion sites.

    Things to keep in mind

    • “Public” includes membership networks anyone can join. Sites with no listed point count are counted as one point.
    • DGT counts vehicles at the owner's tax address. 19 municipalities with fleet registrations (more than 2× median vehicles per resident) have resident EVs re-estimated.
    • Expected supply is a peer benchmark, not true demand: a region-wide shortage would not show up.
    • OpenChargeMap is crowdsourced; recent sites may be missing. Municipality level hides neighbourhood differences, especially in Madrid city.
